<details>
<summary>The problem and the decision</summary>

### The problem

Fall 2025. Zvonobot was a mature outbound calling platform: robots dialing contact bases with pre-recorded scripts. LLMs had just learned to hold a real conversation. Recorded scripts would only get us so far, and if we did not build live agents, someone else would take the category. The question was not whether to build voice AI, but how to find out fast and cheaply whether our customers would pay for it.

Why outbound is worth automating for a mid-market B2B team:

- Headcount does not scale linearly: a 30 to 60 seat outbound team is hard to hire, train and keep.
- Speed-to-call decides the funnel: a lead called within minutes converts far better than one called tomorrow, and people are not on every shift.
- Quality is uneven: two operators, one script, two different conversations, and no cheap way to audit what was actually said.
- Lifecycle calls (onboarding nudges, reactivation, NPS, payment reminders) are cheap one by one and huge in volume together, more than a human team can justify.

### The decision

Instead of building a platform first, we went to market on a **white-label voice AI platform in a few weeks**. That put a real product in front of real customers at almost no engineering cost and answered the only question that mattered: do they pay, and do they come back.

They did. On warm lead lists the AI agents consistently beat the legacy robocalls on conversion to the target action, and the first customers renewed. With those numbers, not with a deck, I made the case for an in-house platform: per-call-type margins and repeat payments showed where white-label economics capped us and what owning the stack would return.

</details>

<details>
<summary>How the product works</summary>

One console for every role: agent assembly, dialing, billing and analytics.

- **Signup without friction.** Email, password, phone, and the client is in. The phone is verified inside the console before the first call, not at the door; Yandex ID login works too.
- **Agent builder.** Voice, model, language, a structured prompt, a contact base and a phone number: a working outbound agent in minutes.
- **Campaigns.** A campaign is "this agent calls this base from this number". Pause, resume, retry, switch carriers without leaving the page.
- **Call history.** Every call lands with status, outcome, duration, cost and a recording. Numbers are masked, so the console is safe to demo and audit.
- **A client never goes negative mid-campaign.** A campaign holds its estimated cost up front, settles the real cost after every call and releases the hold if it stops early.
- **No call gets stuck, every minute is billed once.** A background poller reconciles every unfinished call with the provider every 60 seconds and settles the wallet.
- **Post-call analytics.** An LLM breaks each conversation into an outcome, a sentiment and custom parameters. The dashboard ranks outcomes by frequency and shows the sentiment split and the hours when people actually answer.
- **Four roles, one codebase.** Super-admin, manager, client owner, accountant, each with its own scope and views.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Under the hood</summary>

- **Frontend:** React 19 + TypeScript + Vite, vanilla CSS with strict design tokens, `data-theme="dark"` swap, a sidebar that adapts to the role.
- **Backend:** Flask modular monolith, SQLAlchemy + Alembic, PostgreSQL for state, Redis for jobs.
- **Voice + LLM:** dialog orchestration on top of a voice provider's execution layer; voice, ASR, TTS and the LLM are configurable per agent.
- **Telephony:** SIP trunk for Russian numbers, operator detection via HLR lookup, so a number is known to be reachable before it is dialed.

### Three product decisions I would defend again

1. **Reconcile, do not trust webhooks.** Providers drop lifecycle events. The 60-second poller is what makes "every minute billed exactly once" a fact rather than a hope.
2. **Hold, then settle.** Estimated cost is held at campaign start and the real cost is settled per call, with a row lock so two concurrent calls cannot spend the same remaining balance.
3. **Ingest that survives real CSVs.** Client bases arrive as `+7 (982)…`, `8982…`, numbers in the wrong column. Everything normalizes to a canonical form, deduplicates and gets an operator lookup, and flaky carrier routes are dropped by default. This started as my `bazabot` side project and moved into the product when every campaign turned out to need it.

</details>

<details>
<summary>How I worked</summary>

I owned the line: what got built, what it cost, whom we sold it to, pricing and per-call-type margins. Specs, priorities and review were mine. When a hypothesis needed a working answer faster than a sprint, I prototyped it myself with AI coding agents (Claude Code, Codex) and handed the team the learning, not the code.

- The loop: **hypothesis → smallest surface → live customers → decision**, in days, not quarters. White-label first was that loop applied to the whole line.
- Boring infrastructure is product work: holds, reconciliation, idempotency and masked personal data decide whether a SaaS feels stable or fragile.
- Start with the smallest stable surface and grow it. The console started with one role and one report; now it has four roles, billing, an agent builder, analytics and a real call pipeline.
